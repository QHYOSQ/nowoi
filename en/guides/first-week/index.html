FIELD GUIDE 06 / Launch & maintain

The first week after your website goes live

Treat launch as the start of observation: verify real journeys, then let evidence shape the next change.

NOWOI / FIELD NOTES8 min readFirst edition 2026.11.08

A small reference site finally has a public address. Its homepage opens, but do old bookmarks, keyboard navigation, and mobile exercises work too? The first week is a chance to establish a short maintenance loop: observe, reproduce, change, and check again. This suggested schedule is adjustable. It promises no indexing deadline, traffic growth, or advertising approval.

What you will take away

  • Check production pages, moved URLs, and nonexistent URLs directly.
  • Complete real keyboard and mobile journeys and record conditions.
  • Search verification and indexing do not guarantee AdSense approval.
  • Distinguish verified fixes, pending checks, and unreproduced reports.
01

01 / Capture a baseline before announcing

List the homepage, a guide, an exercise, a contact page, and a deliberately nonexistent address. Enter each URL directly rather than always navigating from home. This exposes deployment problems with deep links, refreshes, and error handling.

If /notes/image-size.html permanently moved to /en/guides/image-budget/, redirect it to that relevant replacement. Permanent moves commonly use 301 or 308; verify the hosting rule. Do not redirect every unknown address to the homepage.

Check the production domain. Record date, address, browser, and result; do not mark an unperformed checklist as passed. The document request in the network panel reveals the response beyond the visible design.

02

02 / Day one: complete one whole task

Choose a goal: find the image-budget guide, change an exercise, and return to the directory. Put the mouse aside. Use Tab and Shift+Tab, checking visible focus, sensible order, and working controls. For overlays, check dismissal and where focus returns.

Repeat on a phone, including menus, tables, code, and results. Narrow-screen emulation is useful when hardware is unavailable, but label it as emulation. Probe around 360 CSS pixels and with browser zoom: are buttons hidden, or ordinary paragraphs unnecessarily scrollable sideways?

Open two guides in separate tabs and check that their titles distinguish them accurately. Improve unclear action feedback before adding decoration. This journey is a basic accessibility screen, not a comprehensive audit.

03

03 / Days two and three: inspect search visibility

After verifying the appropriate Search Console property, inspect representative full URLs. Indexed information describes Google's known version; the live test helps assess the current page's potential indexability. A successful live test does not mean a page is already indexed.

Review retrieved content, resources, and status. A submitted sitemap should list intended public, working canonical addresses. Change redirects, robots rules, or noindex directives in response to a diagnosed problem rather than removing every restriction because a page is absent.

Google explicitly states that a 200 response does not guarantee indexing. An indexing request does not promise immediate inclusion either. Search Console addresses search visibility; AdSense applies its own site and policy review. Verification or indexing cannot promise advertising approval.

04

04 / Days three to five: seek reproducible feedback

“On this page, I tried this action, expected this result, and saw this instead” is more useful than “the site is broken.” Offer a contact route and invite that information. Ask for synthetic reproductions instead of real contracts, credentials, or private files.

Prioritize blocked tasks. An export that does nothing usually matters more than a spacing preference. Reproduce it first: is the failure specific to a browser, privacy setting, or input? Keep the smallest sample, exact steps, and visible error.

If feedback is absent, repeat your own journey or invite a willing tester. Do not fill the gap with invented testimonials, usage counts, or success rates. No evidence yet is a legitimate status.

05

05 / Days six and seven: leave a useful maintenance record

Record the problem, affected journey, change, and verification. This is an illustrative entry, not a completed test:

Problem: closing the mobile menu loses keyboard focus. Journey: guide → open menu → close. Change: return focus to the menu button. Check: repeat in the named browser; other browsers remain untested.

A fixed status needs repeated steps behind it. Mark untested environments as pending. For a failure you cannot reproduce, record attempted conditions rather than forcing a conclusion.

Recheck related paths after changes: a shared menu affects many pages, while a corrected example may need a narrower review. Prioritize the remaining work for next week. Public change notes can explain improvements without disclosing user inputs, private addresses, or secrets captured in errors.

06

06 / Keep the loop repeatable

Use the launch exercise to organize tasks and verification. It is a checklist, not a remote site scanner or a Google submission service. Mark a check complete only after performing it.

End the week with trustworthy facts: working addresses, blocked actions, resolved feedback, and remaining questions. Green scores do not replace those facts. Neither does adding unrelated articles simply to pursue advertising approval.

Repeat the same loop whenever guides, dependencies, or features change: visit, operate, observe, modify, verify. For a small site, resolving one understood problem is easier to maintain than publishing many unverified additions.
